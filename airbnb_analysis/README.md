## Fact 1:
Although Clintonville is the most expensive neighborhood in Columbus among those with at least 20 Airbnbs ($47.13 per person for a family of four), it is closer in price to the second cheapest neighborhood (South Linden, $41.00) than South Linden is to the cheapest (North Linden, $32.55). North Linden may be considered a relatively undesirable neighborhood compared to the rest.

## Fact 2:
There is not much variation amongst cities when comparing the percentage of entire home/apartment listings that are currently available less than half the month. While Columbus has the lowest percentage (19.0%), Chicago, the highest, has only 21.8% under these conditions.

## Fact 3:
Total reviews (and presumably total Airbnb demand) in the Twin Cities seems to be much higher in warmer, summer months than other months. Despite being a colder month, April 2026 had over 1200 more reviews for properties in the Twin Cities than May 2026. This leads me to believe that events such as Easter may have risen activity.

## Fact 4:
Unlike in the Twin Cities, there seems to be a sizeable Superhost premium for listings in both Chicago ($43.75 premium) and Columbus ($40.50), at least when comparing median listing price. This implies a possibility that the Twin Cities may be a unique Airbnb market in this regard.

## Fact 5:
Chicago loses the most listings that have no rows in availability, with 267. This does line up with outside knowledge of relative market sizes.
